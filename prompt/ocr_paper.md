Transcribe this page of a scientific paper completely and exactly as it is printed. Copy every word, every symbol, every number. Omit nothing, add nothing, translate nothing, and explain nothing. The answer is the page and only the page: no preamble, no closing remark, no note about what you did, and no code fence around the whole of it.

- The answer is Markdown. A heading is marked with hashes and keeps the number the page prints in front of it, so a section headed "2. DATA DEPENDENCIES" is written `## 2. DATA DEPENDENCIES` and a subsection under it is `### 2.1. Interrelation Dependencies`. Do not invent a level the page does not print and do not promote the first paragraph of a section to a heading.
- There is no HTML in the answer. Not a `<table>`, not a `<br>`, not a `<sub>` or a `<sup>`, not an `<i>` or a `<b>`, not an `&nbsp;` or an `&amp;`. A subscript or a superscript on a symbol is mathematics and is written `$x_i$` or `$2^n$`; one in a name the page prints in running text, as in H₂O, is written with the character the page prints. Emphasis is `*italic*` and `**bold**`. A tag in the answer is a tag a reader sees as a tag, and one page of it costs the whole page its transcription.
- A paragraph is one line. The line breaks inside a printed paragraph are the width of the column and nothing else, so join those lines with a single space and break the line only where the paragraph itself ends. A word broken across two lines with a hyphen is one word, written whole and without the hyphen, unless the hyphen belongs to the word. A heading, a display formula, a list item, a table row and a footnote each stand on a line of their own in the same way.
- A page set in two or three columns is read one column at a time, the whole of the left column from top to bottom before the first line of the next. Never read a line of one column and then the line beside it. The columns of a page are the hardest thing about this job and a reader that interleaves them produces text that is fluent and says nothing.
- Mathematics is LaTeX. Inline as `$...$`, displayed on its own line as `$$...$$`. A display the paper numbers keeps its number as `\tag{N}` inside the dollars, with the number exactly as printed, so a formula numbered (3.1) ends `\tag{3.1}$$`. Do not number a display the page leaves unnumbered.
- A dollar sign that is money is written `\$`. A bare `$` opens mathematics, and a copyright line that reads "0000-0000/98/0000-0000 $00.00" costs the page its whole transcription.
- Where the journal prints a running head above the text block, transcribe it verbatim on the first line, whatever it says, and where it prints none do not invent one. The number printed on the page goes on a line of its own with nothing else on that line, in the place the page prints it: first if it is at the head, last if it is at the foot. Last means last: a page whose number is at the foot ends on that number, under the footnotes and under everything else, because a Markdown footnote is written at the end of the answer wherever the page sets it and a number written above one is a number no longer at the foot of anything. Both the head and the number are furniture and both are taken out later by a program that has to see them first. The page map depends on that number, so do not tidy it, do not renumber it and do not move it.
- Do not transcribe the text inside a figure, a diagram, a plot or a photograph. The words on the axes of a graph and the labels inside the boxes of a block diagram are part of the picture, and read as prose they come out as word soup in the middle of a paragraph. Write the caption and nothing else: on its own line, starting with the label as the page prints it, as in `Figure 1. Response of the detector against the applied field.` A figure with no caption is written as `Figure.` on its own line.
- A table whose cells form a plain grid is a Markdown pipe table, with its caption on a line of its own in the place the page prints it. A table that a pipe table cannot carry, because its headers span or stack or because half of it is a drawing, is written as the page lays it out inside a fenced block tagged `text`, with the caption in the same place. A pipe table with the wrong number of columns is worse than no table at all: if in doubt, use the fence. Never line a table up with spaces outside a fence. Columns held apart by runs of spaces in ordinary text are a table nothing downstream can read and a paragraph no person can read, so they are either a pipe table or they are inside a `text` fence, and there is no third answer. Every row of a pipe table has the same number of pipes as its header, the empty cells included.
- Anything the page sets in a monospaced face or lays out by its indentation goes in a fence of three backticks, always, however short it is. That is a program listing, a pseudocode or algorithm block with its numbered steps, a grammar, a type rule, a protocol message, a command line, a configuration file, a file format and a worked trace. Tag the fence with the language: `algol`, `lisp`, `fortran`, `c`, `python`, `go`, `sql`, or `text` where the listing has no language anybody names. Every space of the indentation is kept as printed. Do not reformat it, do not close a bracket the listing leaves open and do not fix what looks like a typo: a published listing with a bug in it is a published listing with a bug in it. A listing run into the prose as a paragraph is the commonest way this page can be got wrong, and it destroys the one thing about a listing that carries meaning, which is its shape.
- A footnote is a Markdown footnote and not the rule and the small type the page sets. Put `[^1]`, `[^2]` in the body where the page puts its mark, numbered from one on this page whatever sign the page uses, and put the note itself at the foot as `[^1]:` and its text, one to a line.
- A bibliography entry is one line, exactly as printed, with the label the page prints kept in front of it, whether that is `1.`, `[1]`, `(1)` or the author's surname. Do not renumber, do not reorder, do not expand an abbreviated journal name and do not turn a citation into a link.
- Keep the language, the spelling and the notation of the page. An old spelling, an obsolete symbol, an author's own notation and a mistake the author made are all transcribed as printed. You are copying a document and not correcting one.
- Where the page is genuinely unreadable, write ⟨illegible⟩ at that point and nothing else. Never guess a digit, a subscript, an index or an author's name. One unreadable word is one marker, not a sentence explaining that the scan is poor.
