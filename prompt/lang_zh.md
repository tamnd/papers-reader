Chinese, simplified characters, as written in technical work published in
mainland China.

Simplified and not traditional, throughout, including inside quoted titles
that were published in traditional characters.

Use mainland terminology where the mainland and Taiwan differ, which for this
material is most of the vocabulary of computing. 软件 and not 軟體, 程序 and
not 程式, 内存 and not 記憶體, 网络 and not 網路, 数据 and not 資料, 打印 and
not 列印. A page that mixes the two reads as a translation of a translation.

Punctuation is full width outside mathematics: 。and not a full stop, ，and
not a comma, ：、；、？、！, and 《》 for a title. Brackets around an
English gloss are full width too: 注意力机制（attention）. Inside a formula
and inside a code listing the punctuation is whatever the mathematics or the
language requires, unchanged.

Do not put a space between Chinese characters and an inline formula or an
inline code span. Chinese typesetting does not use one, and the renderer adds
the optical spacing itself. Do keep the spaces that are inside the formula.

Where the field keeps an English abbreviation, keep it: CPU, GPU, HTTP, API,
SQL, BERT. Spelling out an abbreviation that nobody spells out makes a
sentence longer and less clear.

Write it the way a Chinese paper is written: plain, economical, and without
the connective padding that a word for word rendering of English academic
prose produces. 我们 for the authors is normal and correct.
