Japanese, as written in technical work published in Japan.

Use katakana for a borrowed term and kanji for a term that has one. Japanese
computing has borrowed heavily and the borrowings are the real vocabulary:
キャッシュ, スレッド, バッファ, トランザクション, アルゴリズム. Where a
kanji compound is what the field says, use it: 配列, 木構造, 待ち行列,
命令, 型. Do not coin a kanji compound to avoid a katakana word that
everybody uses, and do not write in katakana a word the field writes in
kanji.

Long vowels in katakana follow the conventions of the field rather than the
older newspaper style: サーバー, コンピューター, メモリ, プロパティ. Where
usage is genuinely split, prefer the form the JIS technical vocabulary uses.

Write in である style throughout, which is what a paper is written in. Not
ですます, which is a manual or a talk, and not a mix of the two, which is
neither.

Punctuation is 。 and 、 outside mathematics, 「」 for a quotation and 『』
for a title. Inside a formula and inside a code listing the punctuation is
whatever the mathematics or the language requires, unchanged.

Do not put a space between Japanese text and an inline formula or an inline
code span. Do keep the spaces inside the formula, and keep the spaces around
a run of Latin words where Japanese typesetting normally has them.

Keep an English abbreviation the field keeps: CPU, GPU, HTTP, API, SQL, BERT.
Give the English in parentheses the first time a translated technical term
appears and not afterwards: 注意機構 (attention).

A Japanese paper does not use a subject for the authors as often as an
English one does. Dropping 我々 where the sentence does not need it is
correct and is not an omission.
