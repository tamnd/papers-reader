This page is from Codd, A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks, Communications of the ACM, volume 13 number 6, June 1970. The following are about this paper and this printing of it.

- There is no running head. The journal prints a running foot instead, two pieces on one line at the two edges of the page: the words Communications of the ACM with the page number beside them at the outer edge, and Volume 13 / Number 6 / June, 1970 at the inner edge. The number is in the high 300s and it goes on a line of its own, as the last line. The first page also carries a department line above the title, Information Retrieval at the left and the name of the editor at the right, and that line is transcribed on the first line as the running head it stands in for.
- A numbered section head is set on a line of its own and is a heading. The top level is set in bold in title case and is written `## 1. Relational Model and Normal Form`. The level under it is set in small capitals and is written in capitals as it reads, `### 1.1. INTRODUCTION`.
- The third level is not a heading. 1.2.1, 1.2.2 and 1.2.3 are run-in heads: the number and an italic title sit at the start of the first paragraph of the passage and the prose carries straight on from them on the same line. Transcribe them where they are, as `1.2.2. *Indexing Dependence.* In the context of formatted data, an index is ...`, and do not lift them onto a line of their own or put hashes in front of them.
- Structures 1 to 5 are not tables in the sense a pipe table means. They are three labelled columns, File, Segment and Fields, where most cells are empty and the indentation of the field names under a segment is what carries the meaning. Put each one in a fence tagged `text`, laid out as the page lays it out, with its caption line above it as printed.
- Figures 1 and 2 are not pictures and the rule about not transcribing what is inside a figure does not apply to them. They are relations printed as arrays of numbers, and the numbers are the content of the page. Transcribe each as a table, with the relation name and the parenthesised list of domains as the page sets them, and put the caption under it as printed, beginning `FIG. 1.` with the label in the form the page uses.
- The single italic letters are mathematics and are written as mathematics: `$R$` for a relation, `$S_1$, $S_2$, ..., $S_n$` for the sets, `$P$` for a program, `$n$-tuple` and `$n$-ary` for the words the paper hyphenates that way. A subscript is set small and low and is a subscript.
- The notation is Codd's own and it predates the one every textbook since has used. He writes about projection, join and composition in words and in his own array notation. There is no σ, no π and no ⋈ anywhere in this paper: do not introduce one, and do not rewrite a relation with named attributes as the `R(A, B)` of a later account.
- The number sign in `part #`, `project #` is how the journal abbreviates the word number. Keep it as printed.
