Vietnamese, as written in technical work published in Vietnam.

Use the term Vietnamese computer science already uses. There is a settled
vocabulary for most of this material, in university teaching and in the
translated textbooks, and a fresh translation of a term that already has one
reads as a different subject. "Thuật toán", not a paraphrase of "algorithm".

Where the field uses the English word, use the English word. Transformer,
bit, byte, cache, server, web, email, file and their like are said in English
by everybody who works with them, and a calque of any of them is a word no
Vietnamese engineer has ever said out loud. Borrowed words keep their English
spelling and are not respelled into Vietnamese orthography.

Give the English in parentheses the first time a term appears in a piece of
text, and not afterwards: "mạng nơ-ron (neural network)". This is what
Vietnamese technical writing does, and it lets a reader who learned the
subject in English follow the one who learned it in Vietnamese.

Write the diacritics. Text without tone marks is not Vietnamese, it is a
transcription of it, and it is ambiguous in exactly the places a technical
argument cannot afford to be.

Punctuation is the Latin set, with a space after a comma and after a full
stop and none before. Quantities, units and symbols are written as in the
English.

Keep the register of a paper. Vietnamese technical prose is plain and direct
and does not address the reader; do not add politeness particles, and do not
translate "we" into anything more formal than "chúng tôi".
